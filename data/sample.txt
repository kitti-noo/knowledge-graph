Marie Curie was born in Warsaw in 1867. She moved to Paris and studied physics at the University of Paris, where she met Pierre Curie. Marie Curie married Pierre Curie in 1895.

Marie Curie and Pierre Curie discovered polonium and radium. Polonium was named after Poland, Marie Curie's home country. In 1903, Marie Curie, Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel shared the Nobel Prize in Physics for their research on radioactivity. Henri Becquerel had discovered radioactivity in 1896.

In 1911, Marie Curie won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the discovery of polonium and radium. She was the first person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences.

Irène Joliot-Curie was the daughter of Marie Curie and Pierre Curie. She married Frédéric Joliot, and together they discovered artificial radioactivity. Irène Joliot-Curie and Frédéric Joliot won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1935.

Marie Curie founded the Radium Institute in Paris. Irène Joliot-Curie later became director of the Radium Institute.
